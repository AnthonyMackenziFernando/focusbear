# Cyber Security Guidelines

## What I Learned

I learned that cyber security is not only about technical security systems. Everyday actions such as using strong passwords, locking devices, checking links, and protecting accounts also play an important role in keeping company and user information secure.

Working remotely can introduce additional risks such as phishing emails, malicious links, insecure Wi-Fi, stolen devices, weak passwords, and accidentally sharing confidential information.

## Security Checks I Completed

As part of this task, I checked my own device and account security settings.

### Device Lock

I checked the automatic lock settings on my computer and phone.

- Computer auto-lock: **5 minutes**
- Phone auto-lock: **2 minutes**
- Both devices require authentication again after locking.

I changed the settings where necessary so that my devices do not remain unlocked when I step away from them.

### Password Security

I reviewed how I store my passwords and checked that my important work accounts use unique passwords.

I use **the password manager built into my browser** to manage my passwords.

I did not include or record any actual passwords as part of this task.

### Two-Factor Authentication

I checked the 2FA settings for my important work accounts.

2FA is enabled on:

- **Google account**
- **Focus Bear account**

Where 2FA was not enabled, I enabled it where the account and company policy allowed me to do so.

I did not record or share any authentication codes, backup codes, passwords, or other sensitive information.

## Phishing and Suspicious Links

Phishing is one of the common risks when working remotely. I should not automatically trust an email or message just because it appears to come from someone I know.

Before clicking a link or opening an attachment, I should check the sender, the link destination, and whether the request makes sense. If something seems unusual, such as an unexpected login request, password reset, or urgent request for information, I should verify it through another trusted method.

## My New Security Habit

One security habit I will follow during my internship is to **lock my computer whenever I step away from my desk**, even for a short period.

I will also make checking links and unexpected requests a normal part of my routine instead of clicking first and thinking about the security implications afterwards.

## Reflection

The main thing I learned is that good cyber security comes from consistent habits. Technical security controls are important, but they can still be undermined by simple mistakes such as reusing passwords, leaving a device unlocked, or clicking a suspicious link.

Completing the security checks made the task more practical because I was able to review my own settings rather than only reading about recommended security practices. I also became more aware of how device locking, password management, and 2FA work together to protect work accounts and information.

Going forward, I will keep these settings enabled and continue checking my account and device security when needed.
