# Data Privacy Reflection

## What I Learned from the Privacy Policy

After reviewing Focus Bear's Privacy Policy, I learned that the application handles more types of personal data than I initially expected. This includes basic identifying information such as email addresses and phone numbers, as well as technical information such as the user's OS and Device type too.

Focus Bear can also process information related to user's habits and lifestyle. Some habits may potentially reveal sensitive information about a person's health or religious beliefs. The policy also mentions that, the users can choose to send detailed logs when reporting a problem.

I also learned that Focus Bear works with several third-party service providers for things such as authentication, cloud services, payments, communication, analytics and other technical services. This means protecting user's data is important not only within the application itself, but also when data is handled through these services also.

## Handling Confidential Data

As an intern,When working on security-related tasks across information such as user details, application logs, security configurations, internal policies, audit findings and company documents. I have to treat this information as confidential unless it is clearly intended to be shared publicly.

Some practices I can follow are:

- Only access information that is necessary for the task I am working on.
- Avoid copying sensitive information into unnecessary files or messages.
- Do not share internal information with people who are not authorised to access it.
- Use approved company systems when storing or sharing work-related information.
- Avoid including real user information in screenshots, reports or examples when it is not necessary.
- Double-check recipients before sending files or messages containing sensitive information.
- Delete temporary copies of sensitive information when they are no longer needed.

## Handling a Suspected Data Breach

If I accidentally share confidential information or notice something that could be a data breach, I should not try to hide or fix the issue. Must need to inform the Boss.

I should report it to the relevant supervisor or security contact as soon as possible, explain what happened, what information may have been exposed, and who may have received it. I should also preserve relevant evidence instead of deleting it.

## My Security Practice

One habit I will adopt is, follow the principle of least access when working with internal information. I will only access or copy the data that is actually required for the task.

I will also avoid using real user information in my notes or reports whenever it is not necessary. I will use examples, anonymised information or placeholders instead.

## Reflection

The main thing I learned is that confidential information is not limited to passwords or financial information. Even something like a user's habits, device information, application logs or internal security documentation can contain information that should be protected.

Going forward, I will be more careful about where I store, share and discuss information obtained during my security tasks. I will also report any accidental disclosure or suspected security issue rather than handle it on my own.
